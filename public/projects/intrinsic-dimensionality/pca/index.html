How it works

Read the covariance spectrum as a soft count of important axes. This measures effective linear dimension, which can differ from manifold dimension.

DPR=(∑iλi)2∑iλi2

λᵢ are the eigenvalues of the centred sample covariance. Equal variance across D axes gives a participation ratio of D.

  1. Centre the data.Subtract the sample mean and compute the covariance in the full coordinate space.
  2. Inspect the spectrum.Each eigenvalue measures variance along a principal axis.
  3. Count effective directions.The participation ratio weights axes by variance. The 95% rank instead counts axes needed for 95% of total variance.

The version in this notebook

PCA is global and linear here. The lab reports the participation ratio and the 95% explained-variance rank; neither is a nonlinear manifold-dimension estimator.

What to watch for

A circle has intrinsic dimension 1 but usually has a participation ratio near 2. A curved sheet can occupy three linear directions. Changing feature scales also changes the spectrum.
