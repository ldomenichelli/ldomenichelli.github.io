DThe space around the data

Ambient dimension counts the coordinates you record. A point in 3D has three, whatever shape the data follows.

dThe shape within the data

Intrinsic dimension describes the local degrees of freedom. Along a curve, one parameter can be enough.

rThe scale you look at

Zoom in and noise can look like extra dimensions. Zoom out and a neighbourhood can span different folds.

02 / Meet the methods

Five ways to look closer.

Each method has its own assumptions, diagnostic plot and worked explanation.

Your experiment travels with you

Levina–Bickel MLE

Zoom in around one point. The spacing of its neighbours tells us how quickly the local volume fills.

Explore the method →

Expected simplex skewness

Nearby vectors become less aligned as dimension grows. ESS translates their geometric spread into a local estimate.

Explore the method →

Two nearest neighbours

For each point, compare its first two neighbours. Their distance ratios reveal a dimension through a simple fitted line.

Explore the method →

Correlation dimension

As a ball grows, more pairs fit inside it. The scaling of that count gives a view of the geometry across distance scales.

Explore the method →

PCA participation ratio

Read the covariance spectrum as a soft count of important axes. This measures effective linear dimension, which can differ from manifold dimension.

Explore the method →

Different answers can be informative.

A circle needs one parameter to trace, but its variance spreads over two axes. A local manifold estimator and a global linear estimator are asking different questions.

Try changing one control at a time. Keep the sample fixed while varying the neighbourhood, then regenerate to see how much sampling matters.
