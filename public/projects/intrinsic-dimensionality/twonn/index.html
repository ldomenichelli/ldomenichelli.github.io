How it works

For each point, compare its first two neighbours. Their distance ratios reveal a dimension through a simple fitted line.

μ=r2r1−log(1−F(μ))=dlogμ

r₁ and r₂ are the first two neighbour distances; F is the cumulative distribution of their ratio.

  1. Keep only two neighbours.Compute μ = r₂ / r₁ for every valid sample.
  2. Order the ratios.Use their empirical cumulative distribution to form the log–log coordinates.
  3. Fit through the origin.The fitted slope is the dimension estimate. The plotted points show how well that relation holds.

The version in this notebook

The fit uses the lowest 90% of the sorted ratios, following the paper’s tail-trimming convention. The empirical CDF is formed before trimming. No k setting enters this estimate.

What to watch for

Duplicates invalidate ratios. Noise at the nearest-neighbour scale, small samples and sharp boundaries can change the slope; a straight-looking fit is not a guarantee.

Try this

Compare a circle with a plane at zero noise. Then add noise to the circle and see what the two nearest neighbours resolve.
