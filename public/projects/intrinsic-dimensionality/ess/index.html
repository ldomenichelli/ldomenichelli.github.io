How it works

Nearby vectors become less aligned as dimension grows. ESS translates their geometric spread into a local estimate.

s^=∑i<j‖vi∧vj‖∑i<j‖vi‖‖vj‖

The vectors are centred on the neighbourhood mean. The wedge magnitude measures a parallelogram’s area.

  1. Centre the neighbourhood.Take a point and its k neighbours, then subtract their common centroid.
  2. Measure each vector pair.Compare the parallelogram area with the product of the two vector lengths.
  3. Read the calibration curve.Match the weighted pair statistic to the theoretical dimension curve.

The version in this notebook

This is the two-vector ESSa statistic. The calibration is s(d) = Γ(d/2)² / [Γ((d+1)/2)Γ((d−1)/2)], with s(1) = 0. The lab inverts it continuously and averages valid local dimensions.

What to watch for

Directional imbalance, curvature and noise can affect this calibration. An estimate beyond the calibration range is reported as unavailable, rather than silently capped.

Try this

Choose a ball and raise its dimension. Inspect a vector pair: the displayed projection can look aligned even when the full-dimensional vectors are not.
