\section*{TPR representation}

The hidden state is

\[
  h \in \R^{4096}.
\]

After TPRization, the representation is arranged as

\[
  u
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    u_{\mathrm{agent}} \\
    u_{\mathrm{patient}} \\
    u_{\mathrm{roles}}
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  u \in \R^{3 \times 32} \cong \R^{96}.
\]

The linear readout is

\[
  u = Ah + c,
  \qquad
  A \in \R^{96 \times 4096}.
\]

\section*{Desired activation change}

For the agent component, the notebook considers the change

\[
  \Delta u_{\mathrm{agent}}
  = C_{\mathrm{prisoner}} - C_{\mathrm{baby}}.
\]

Keeping the patient and role components unchanged gives

\[
  \Delta u
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    C_{\mathrm{prisoner}} - C_{\mathrm{baby}} \\
    0 \\
    0
  \end{bmatrix}.
\]

\section*{Hidden-state update}

The corresponding hidden-state displacement satisfies

\[
  \Delta u = A\,\Delta h.
\]

Using the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse,

\[
  \Delta h
  = A^{+}\Delta u
  = A^{\mathsf T}\!\left(AA^{\mathsf T}\right)^{-1}\Delta u.
\]

There can be several displacements

\[
  \Delta h_1,\ \Delta h_2,\ \ldots
  \qquad\text{such that}\qquad
  A\,\Delta h_i = \Delta u.
\]

The pseudoinverse selects the minimum-norm solution:

\[
  \Delta h^{A}
  =
  \underset{\Delta h}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}
  \lVert \Delta h \rVert_2
  \qquad
  \text{subject to}
  \qquad
  A\,\Delta h = \Delta u.
\]

The notebook also records

\[
  AA^{+} \simeq I_{96}.
\]

Finally, the steered hidden state is

\[
  h_{\mathrm{steered}} = h + \alpha\,\Delta h.
\]
