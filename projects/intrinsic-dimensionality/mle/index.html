How it works

Zoom in around one point. The spacing of its neighbours tells us how quickly the local volume fills.

d^=k−1∑j=1k−1logTkTj

Tⱼ is the distance to neighbour j; Tₖ is the neighbourhood radius.

  1. Choose a neighbourhood.Sort the distances from the selected point to its k nearest neighbours.
  2. Measure relative spacing.Compare each inner radius with the outer radius through log(Tₖ / Tⱼ).
  3. Invert and aggregate.Estimate each point locally, then take the arithmetic mean of the valid estimates.

The version in this notebook

The displayed MLE uses the k − 1 numerator and an arithmetic mean of local estimates. Other bias corrections and aggregation rules produce different values.

What to watch for

Large neighbourhoods can cross bends or density changes. Very small ones are noisy; duplicate points make some distance ratios undefined.

Try this

Start with a line and no noise. Add a little noise, then increase k: the scale you inspect changes what the estimator sees.
